How to play chess

Make your first move

White goes first, then players take turns. Select one of your pieces to see its legal moves, then select a highlighted square. Landing on an opponent’s piece captures it. Most pieces stop when they reach another piece. The knight can jump over pieces.

How the pieces move

King
Moves one square in any direction. It must stay out of attack.
Queen
Moves any number of open squares straight or diagonally.
Rook
Moves any number of open squares horizontally or vertically.
Bishop
Moves any number of open squares diagonally.
Knight
Jumps in an L shape, two squares one way and one to the side.
Pawn
Moves one square straight forward, or two from its starting square if both are clear. Captures one square diagonally forward.

Check and checkmate

Check means your king is under attack. You must escape by moving the king, capturing the attacker, or blocking the attack when possible. If your king is in check and you have no legal move, that’s checkmate and your opponent wins. If you have no legal move and your king is safe, that’s stalemate, a draw.

Special moves

  • Castling. Move the king two squares toward a rook. The rook moves to the square the king crossed. Both pieces must be on their starting squares and have never moved. The squares between them must be empty, and the king can’t castle out of, through, or into check.
  • Promotion. When a pawn reaches the far edge of the board, choose a queen, rook, bishop, or knight.
  • En passant. If an enemy pawn moves two squares from its starting square and lands beside your pawn, you can capture it as if it had moved one square. You have to do it on your very next move.
